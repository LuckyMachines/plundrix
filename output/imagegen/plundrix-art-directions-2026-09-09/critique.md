# Art-Direction Critique

## Final synthesis pass

`11-recommended-synthesis-final.png` is the recommended visual target. It keeps the current game's readable turn model while applying the new material and composition system: one dominant vault and route map, a real information-bearing dossier, abstract masked crew identities, a shallow gadget rail, three tactile action plates, and one warm Confirm Move ticket. The initial synthesis was rejected and corrected because it introduced animal portraits and used navigation where turn actions belonged.

## Decision

The new work validates the direction. Plundrix should become a tactile caper interface, not a dark dashboard with themed accents. The strongest answer is not one screen copied literally; it is a product grammar assembled from all four studies.

Recommended synthesis:

1. Use the Heist Desk Hub as the product-wide shell and navigation model.
2. Use the Living Vault as the signature interactive spectacle.
3. Use the Crooked Dossier as the progression and history grammar.
4. Use the Crew Briefing Table as the multiplayer state grammar.
5. Preserve the current product's strongest qualities: readable typography, real text, responsive structure, accessibility, and restrained animation.

## 06 - Living Vault

What works:

- The vault finally owns the screen. Its scale answers the old problem immediately.
- Five stages are visibly connected, making progression feel mechanical rather than tabular.
- The three decisions have distinct silhouettes and colors before any copy is read.
- Cords create a convincing causal relationship between choice and mechanism.
- Depth evidence shows a useful order: substrate, mechanism, route plates, then foreground controls.

What to refine:

- Reduce the brass and bevel count by about 20 percent. The center is close to ornate steampunk rather than precise caper hardware.
- Replace the left stack of generic fire/heart/loot gauges with Plundrix-specific status instruments.
- Preserve the current route copy and probabilities in code; the art study leaves too little text room.
- Keep only one dossier portrait treatment and align it with the established operator/mask language.
- On mobile, split the composition into stage rail, lock, route plates, and dossier instead of shrinking the board.

Verdict: Best signature-gameplay reference. High impact, high implementation cost.

## 07 - Crooked Dossier

What works:

- The screen has a narrative path rather than a matrix of equal cards.
- The oversized file, stamp trail, rival ledger, gadget drawer, and next-operation ticket each have a clear semantic job.
- The orange ticket produces the cleanest next-action hierarchy in this batch.
- The zero state can remain emotionally rich because empty slots still read as future evidence.
- Depth evidence confirms distinct planes instead of the current screen's nearly uniform slab depth.

What to refine:

- Remove the literal desk lamp and some photographic clutter; they are expensive decoration without product value.
- Do not introduce a new animal cast accidentally. Use existing operator silhouettes, masks, or user-selected insignia.
- Turn the left icon rail into labeled, accessible tabs with tooltips and focus states.
- Simplify the rival ledger to avoid making real data compete with paper texture.
- Reserve thread and pinned-photo motifs for meaningful relationships, not every section.

Verdict: Best emotional and progression reference. Most direct cure for the flat Career screen.

## 08 - Crew Briefing Table

What works:

- It makes multiplayer feel social before the match begins.
- Ready, waiting, open, and locked seats differ through silhouettes, icons, brightness, and color.
- The central route and four-step launch rail turn setup into one continuous ritual.
- `JOIN CREW` is visually unmistakable, while the Sepolia notice is present without dominating.
- Depth evidence shows the launch control and invitation as foreground actions, with the mission plan and seats behind them.

What to refine:

- Separate wallet connection from crew joining; they are distinct product states and should not collapse into one control.
- Make host authority and launch eligibility explicit.
- Avoid relying on large circular portraits alone; expose names and readiness in real text beside each emblem.
- Keep the lever metaphor only if it maps to a real launch interaction and provides keyboard/mobile equivalents.
- Reduce decorative stationery near the bottom corners so the launch rail remains clean.

Verdict: Best interaction-state study. Strongest immediate blueprint for the live lobby.

## 09 - Heist Desk Hub

What works:

- It is the most convincing whole-product identity and the best synthesis of blueprint, dossier, mechanism, and tool tray.
- `PLAY NOW` dominates at a glance without erasing progression, inventory, or social paths.
- The bottom mode tabs feel like one system rather than unrelated cards.
- The partial vault becomes a reusable brand object instead of a one-screen illustration.
- The hub feels mischievous and generous, while the current player hub feels correct but emotionally neutral.

What to refine:

- Replace anthropomorphic character portraits unless Plundrix intentionally commits to that worldbuilding shift. They currently promise a cast and narrative style the product may not deliver.
- Reduce simultaneous detail around the center by 15 to 20 percent; preserve breathing room for dynamic copy and notices.
- Treat the gadget tray as a live loadout/status strip, not a decorative shelf.
- Keep every nav label, player value, and event in the DOM. Only the surrounding frames and textures should be rasterized.
- Define a mobile assembly order: Play Now, current operation, operator progress, crew, tools, then secondary modes.

Verdict: Best overall direction and recommended product shell.

## System rules worth adopting

- One hero object per surface: vault, dossier, crew plan, or primary play ticket.
- One warm action color per viewport; amber means commitment.
- Cyan means route or active mechanism, mint means ready/success, coral means danger/grudge, brass means value or motion.
- Use four depth layers consistently: dark substrate, blueprint/document plane, functional hardware, then active signal or foreground control.
- Every physical material must communicate state or grouping. Decorative clutter has a strict budget.
- Real copy and data remain code-rendered. Generated art supplies frames, textures, emblems, mechanisms, and cutouts.
- Desktop compositions can overlap; mobile compositions must restack into independent modules.
- Prefer a few large, unmistakable silhouettes over many small ornamental details.

## Priority order for a later implementation pass

1. Recompose the player hub around the Heist Desk hierarchy.
2. Build one reusable vault mechanism and stage rail for Vault Run.
3. Replace the Career card grid with dossier, progression trail, and gadget drawer primitives.
4. Rebuild the live lobby around seats, readiness, and a continuous launch rail.
5. Extract shared paper, blueprint, metal, stamp, ticket, and signal tokens into the design system.
6. Produce responsive mobile assemblies and validate real copy at 320, 768, and 1440 pixels.
